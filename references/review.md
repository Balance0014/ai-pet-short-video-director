# Real footage, edit, and learning

Read when real media or audience results are available. Never infer the quality of a generated clip from its prompt.

## Inspect before judging

Record the actual file, duration, resolution, audio presence, and intended story contract. Watch the complete clip with sound when possible; then inspect representative frames and suspected failure intervals. Check pet identity, coat markings, body count, gait, paw contact, and species-appropriate movement. Compare a continuation's first image and sound with the previous accepted ending.

Judge two scopes separately:

- **Source unit:** Did it achieve its assigned action, visible result, and usable handoff?
- **Whole film:** Does the assembled film deliver the approved choice, consequence, climax, and emotional return?

A source unit may pass while the film remains unfinished. Do not call the whole film complete because an intermediate segment has a local payoff.

## Name the defect and action

Use `keep | trim/repair | redesign downstream | regenerate | stop`, with the observed reason. Check whether the flaw came from story or blocking design, foreseeable generation load, a reference conflict, or irreducible model variation. Fix the cause before changing prompt adjectives.

Preserve useful footage when a cut or downstream redesign maintains the approved story. Editing cannot create a missing causal action, explain an unintroduced character, or reveal a result that was never shown. If the same failure repeats, change the shot or mechanism rather than buy more of the same prompt.

## Review the cut and sound

At each join inspect outgoing and incoming action, eyeline, screen direction, scale, energy, prop state, light, and sound bridge. Review the picture muted for causal clarity, sound without picture for missing or unwanted information, then the full mix. Do not use a dissolve or voice-over to disguise an absent story step.

For a release candidate, inspect the complete export, source quality, audio, and technical playback. If a zero-context viewer is available, ask what happened, what the protagonist did, and why it mattered; record their words separately from your own review. Do not claim an outside test occurred when it did not.

## Learn from performance without overclaiming

Record publication time, observation time, and metric age before comparing videos. Distinguish retention, completion, sharing, and character-follow conversion; each answers a different question. Treat one video as evidence about that video, not a platform law. Promote a recurring rule only after repeated observations or a direct production failure that clearly warrants it.
